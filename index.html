THE FOUNDATIONS

Understanding Mental Health

Mental health is an essential part of overall well-being. It shapes how we think, feel, connect with others, and respond to everyday life.

What Is Mental Health?

Mental health is a state of mental well-being that enables people to cope with the stresses of life, realize their abilities, learn and work well, and contribute to their community. It has intrinsic and instrumental value and is a basic human right.

It includes emotional, psychological, and social well-being. Mental health is relevant at every stage of life and can change over time as people experience different circumstances, relationships, and challenges.

Why Is Mental Health Important?

Mental health is the foundation of your overall well-being, shaping how you think, feel, and act every day. It is just as vital as physical health, and the two are deeply connected.

Looking after mental well-being can support daily functioning, learning, relationships, decision-making, and the ability to cope with change. It also helps people recognize when they need rest, practical support, or professional care.

Factors That Can Affect Mental Health

Mental health is influenced by a combination of factors. No single factor explains every person's experience.

Biological Factors

Genetics, physical health, brain development, and other biological influences.

Life Experiences

Major changes, loss, difficult experiences, personal challenges, and everyday pressures.

Social Environment

Family, friendships, school, work, community, safety, and access to support.

Remember: Everyone has mental health. Experiencing emotional difficulties does not make someone weak, and a mental health condition does not define a person's worth. Support and treatment can help.

LEARN WITHOUT JUDGMENT

Common Mental Health Concerns

Anxiety, depression, and stress can affect people in different ways. Understanding them can encourage empathy, early support, and informed conversations.

Anxiety

Anxiety is an emotional and physical response to stress or anticipated future danger, marked by feelings of tension, worry, and physiological arousal.

Feeling anxious from time to time is a common human response. It may become a concern when it is intense, persistent, difficult to manage, or interferes with everyday activities.

Possible Signs

  • Frequent or excessive worry
  • Feeling restless, tense, or on edge
  • Difficulty concentrating or sleeping
  • Rapid heartbeat, sweating, or muscle tension

Helpful Ways to Cope

  • Try slow, comfortable breathing or grounding.
  • Break overwhelming tasks into smaller steps.
  • Reduce avoidable stressors when possible.
  • Talk with someone trusted or seek professional guidance.

Depression

Depression is a common and serious mood disorder that causes persistent feelings of sadness, emptiness, and a loss of interest in activities you once enjoyed.

It can also affect energy, sleep, appetite, concentration, relationships, and daily responsibilities. Depression is not a personal weakness, and effective support and treatment are available.

Possible Signs

  • Persistent sadness, emptiness, or irritability
  • Loss of interest or enjoyment
  • Changes in sleep, appetite, or energy
  • Feelings of hopelessness or difficulty concentrating

Helpful Ways to Seek Support

  • Reach out to a trusted person.
  • Try to maintain small, manageable routines.
  • Speak with a qualified health professional.
  • Ask for help with school, work, or daily tasks.

Stress

Stress is the body's natural physical and emotional reaction to any demand, pressure, or perceived threat.

Stress can sometimes help people respond to challenges. However, prolonged or overwhelming stress may affect mood, sleep, concentration, relationships, and physical well-being.

Common Sources

  • School, work, or financial pressure
  • Family or relationship difficulties
  • Major life changes or uncertainty
  • Many responsibilities without enough time to rest

Helpful Ways to Manage It

  • Identify what is within your control.
  • Prioritize tasks and take short breaks.
  • Protect sleep and make time for recovery.
  • Ask for support instead of handling everything alone.
Important: These descriptions are for general education. They are not a checklist for self-diagnosis. Only a qualified health professional can assess symptoms and recommend appropriate care. If symptoms persist, worsen, or interfere with daily life, consider seeking professional support.

EVERYDAY WELLNESS

Self-Care and Wellness

Discover simple ways to take care of yourself through healthy habits and daily activities that improve your physical health, emotional balance, and mental well-being.

Daily Self-Care Tips

Learn helpful wellness practices, easy self-care routines, and daily activities that can help you maintain a healthier and more balanced lifestyle. Choose what is realistic for your needs, abilities, and circumstances.

01

Care for Your Body

  • Keep a regular sleep routine when possible.
  • Eat regular, nourishing meals and drink enough water.
  • Choose movement that feels comfortable and accessible.
  • Make time to rest and recover.
02

Care for Your Mind

  • Take short breaks from demanding tasks.
  • Write down thoughts or organize your priorities.
  • Try breathing, grounding, or quiet reflection.
  • Take breaks from overwhelming media when needed.
03

Care for Relationships

  • Stay connected with supportive people.
  • Share how you feel with someone you trust.
  • Set respectful and healthy boundaries.
  • Ask for help when responsibilities feel heavy.
04

Care for Your Environment

  • Create a comfortable place to rest or study.
  • Break chores into manageable tasks.
  • Spend time outdoors when safe and possible.
  • Make space for hobbies and meaningful activities.

A Simple Daily Reset

  1. Pause: Take a moment to notice how you are feeling.
  2. Choose: Pick one small action that could help right now.
  3. Begin: Do that action for a few minutes without expecting perfection.
  4. Reflect: Notice whether it helped and adjust if needed.
Keep it realistic: Self-care is not a replacement for professional treatment, and it should not become another source of pressure. Small steps count, and different approaches work for different people.

YOU ARE NOT ALONE

Help and Resources

Seek reliable organizations, professional assistance, and helpful information that can guide you toward mental health support. Reaching out to someone you trust or a qualified professional can be a meaningful first step.

Where to Begin

Someone You Trust

A trusted friend, family member, teacher, or guidance counselor may listen and help you consider next steps.

Professional Assistance

Psychologists, psychiatrists, counselors, doctors, and other qualified providers can assess concerns and discuss suitable care.

School or Community Support

Ask your school guidance office, local health center, or community services about available support and referrals.
